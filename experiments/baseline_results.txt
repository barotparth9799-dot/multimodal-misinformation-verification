BASELINE COMPARISON RESULTS
Multimodal Misinformation Verification Project

Evaluation Dataset:
15 controlled multimodal test cases

Purpose:
Compare single-modality baselines against the full multimodal verification system.

------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------

1. EVIDENCE-ONLY BASELINE
Accuracy: 0.6000 (60.00%)
Macro F1: 0.6571 (65.71%)

2. IMAGE-ONLY BASELINE
Accuracy: 0.5333 (53.33%)
Macro F1: 0.2319 (23.19%)

3. FULL MULTIMODAL SYSTEM
Accuracy: 0.8667 (86.67%)
Macro F1: 0.8750 (87.50%)

------------------------------------------------------------
COMPARISON
------------------------------------------------------------

The full multimodal system achieved the highest performance.

Accuracy improvement over evidence-only baseline:
86.67% - 60.00% = 26.67 percentage points

Accuracy improvement over image-only baseline:
86.67% - 53.33% = 33.34 percentage points

Macro F1 improvement over evidence-only baseline:
87.50% - 65.71% = 21.79 percentage points

Macro F1 improvement over image-only baseline:
87.50% - 23.19% = 64.31 percentage points

------------------------------------------------------------
INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------

The baseline experiment demonstrates that neither evidence retrieval
alone nor image-text consistency alone performs as well as the
combined multimodal approach.

The full multimodal system combines evidence-based verification,
NLI-based evidence stance analysis, and image-text consistency through
the multimodal fusion and verification pipeline.

On this controlled 15-case evaluation dataset, the combined approach
achieved 86.67% accuracy and 87.50% Macro F1, substantially exceeding
both single-modality baselines.

These results support the project's multimodal design.

IMPORTANT:
These results are from a controlled evaluation dataset and should not
be treated as a general real-world performance guarantee. Additional
realistic and diverse validation is required before final acceptance.
